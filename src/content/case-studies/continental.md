### Project overview
Decades of paper manuals and technical drawings made maintenance, onboarding, and expansion to new sites more difficult. Continental needed to reduce its reliance on undocumented institutional knowledge and give engineers faster access to critical information. We developed a cloud-based documentation system on AWS with search and QR-code access. The initial pilot was designed to reduce time spent locating documentation and improve access to operational knowledge.

### My Role
I conducted on-site research with factory personnel, translated the findings into user personas, and validated them with the people represented in the research.

### Design Process
I interviewed key factory personnel on-site and synthesized the findings into user personas. The personas provided a shared reference as the team defined the product experience, including search and QR-code access. A fellow UX designer created the design system used to build the product.

### Key Learnings
Early on-site research with factory personnel created a shared understanding of user needs and supported smoother collaboration throughout the project. Speaking with the people closest to the work made the resulting insights more relevant.

### Business outcomes
The first factory served as the initial pilot. Early capabilities included:
- Direct access to documentation through search and QR codes
- Structured, searchable onboarding material relying on someone else's memory
- Visibility into document usage and compliance
- Improved multilingual access through translation

A broader rollout across global sites was planned as a future phase.

### Team
The project team included service and UX designers, developers, architects, client technology personnel, and client account stakeholders.
