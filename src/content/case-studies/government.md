### Project overview
The agency had previously attempted to establish a design system, but those efforts never gained real traction — components existed, but teams didn't adopt them into daily work. I came in to understand why and build a system that would gain lasting adoption.

### My Role
I facilitated the creation of the design system from the ground up, working directly with the agency's key stakeholders. Rather than designing in isolation and handing off a finished system, we co-created it together — planning the work jointly and developing the system incrementally, sprint by sprint.

### Design Process
We worked in one-week sprints. Each sprint ended with an evaluation and test period for the components built during that cycle, so nothing shipped into the system without being checked against real use first. That tight feedback loop was central to the approach: short cycles kept the work grounded in what stakeholders actually needed, rather than what looked right on paper.

### Key Learnings
- Short iteration cycles were the difference-maker. Where earlier attempts stalled by trying to define too much upfront, testing components against real work every week kept the system relevant and kept stakeholders engaged.
- Testing went beyond components in isolation. We validated the system by having key stakeholders actually build new screens with it — not just review individual pieces. Combined with the short iterative cycles, that kept us confident we were on the right track throughout.

### Business outcomes
The design system is now used in day-to-day product work by five core contributors. The next phase will explore AI-assisted workflows for maintaining and extending the system.

### Team
I worked as part of a cross-functional team of agency stakeholders, collaborating as one unit throughout the project rather than following separate designer–client tracks.
